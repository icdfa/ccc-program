## Introduction

Welcome to Module 1.4! In this section, we will explore the foundational frameworks that guide cybersecurity practices. Cybersecurity frameworks provide a structured approach to managing cybersecurity risk. They consist of a set of standards, guidelines, and best practices that can be used to assess, implement, and monitor security controls. 

As a cybersecurity consultant, you will need to have a deep understanding of the most common cybersecurity frameworks and be able to apply them effectively to your clients' environments. Let's dive into the three major frameworks you will encounter in the field.

---

## 1. NIST Cybersecurity Framework (CSF)

The NIST Cybersecurity Framework (CSF) is a voluntary framework developed by the National Institute of Standards and Technology (NIST). It provides a high-level, strategic view of cybersecurity risk and is designed to be applicable to organizations of all sizes and sectors.

The CSF is organized into five core functions, which represent the primary pillars of a successful and holistic cybersecurity program:

| Function | Description |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Identify** | Develop an organizational understanding to manage cybersecurity risk to systems, assets, data, and capabilities. This is about knowing what you have and what risks it faces. |
| **Protect** | Develop and implement the appropriate safeguards to ensure delivery of critical infrastructure services. This involves putting defenses in place. |
| **Detect** | Develop and implement the appropriate activities to identify the occurrence of a cybersecurity event. This is about monitoring and finding issues quickly. |
| **Respond** | Develop and implement the appropriate activities to take action regarding a detected cybersecurity event. This covers how you react when an incident happens. |
| **Recover** | Develop and implement the appropriate activities to maintain plans for resilience and to restore any capabilities or services that were impaired due to a cybersecurity event. This is about getting back to normal operations. |

---

## 2. ISO 27001

ISO 27001 is an international standard for information security management. It provides a systematic approach to managing sensitive company information so that it remains secure. It includes people, processes, and IT systems by applying a risk management process.

ISO 27001 is organized into a number of clauses that cover various aspects of security management. Some of the key areas include:

*   **Information security policies:** Establishing the rules of the road.
*   **Organization of information security:** Defining roles and responsibilities.
*   **Human resource security:** Ensuring employees are trustworthy and trained.
*   **Asset management:** Keeping track of what needs protecting.
*   **Access control:** Limiting who can see or use information.
*   **Cryptography:** Using encryption to protect data.
*   **Physical and environmental security:** Securing the actual buildings and hardware.
*   **Operations security:** Managing the day-to-to running of IT systems securely.
*   **Communications security:** Protecting data as it moves across networks.
*   **System acquisition, development and maintenance:** Building security into new systems.
*   **Supplier relationships:** Ensuring third parties also maintain security.
*   **Information security incident management:** Handling security breaches effectively.
*   **Information security aspects of business continuity management:** Keeping the business running during a crisis.
*   **Compliance:** Ensuring all legal and regulatory requirements are met.

## 3. COBIT

COBIT (Control Objectives for Information and Related Technologies) is a framework for the governance and management of enterprise IT. It was developed by ISACA and is designed to help organizations create value from their IT investments while mitigating risks.

COBIT is organized into five main domains, which guide the lifecycle of IT governance and management:

| Domain | Focus Area |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Evaluate, Direct and Monitor (EDM)** | This domain is concerned with the overarching governance of IT, ensuring that stakeholder needs are evaluated, direction is set, and performance is monitored. |
| **Align, Plan and Organize (APO)** | This domain is concerned with the strategic planning and organization of IT to align with business goals. |
| **Build, Acquire and Implement (BAI)** | This domain is concerned with the practical acquisition and implementation of IT solutions and changes. |
| **Deliver, Service and Support (DSS)** | This domain is concerned with the actual delivery and ongoing support of IT services to users. |
| **Monitor, Evaluate and Assess (MEA)** | This domain is concerned with the continuous monitoring and evaluation of IT performance and internal controls. |

## Study Aids & Review

### Glossary of Key Terms
*   **Cybersecurity Framework:** A structured set of guidelines, standards, and best practices to manage and reduce cybersecurity risk.
*   **NIST CSF:** A voluntary framework focusing on five core functions: Identify, Protect, Detect, Respond, and Recover.
*   **ISO 27001:** An international standard detailing requirements for establishing, implementing, maintaining, and continually improving an information security management system (ISMS).
*   **COBIT:** A framework created by ISACA for IT management and IT governance, focusing on aligning IT with business goals.
*   **Risk Management:** The process of identifying, assessing, and controlling threats to an organization's capital and earnings.
